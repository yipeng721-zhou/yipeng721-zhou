I'm a **founder in the analytical instruments industry**, and a close observer of the new kind of creativity AI is unlocking.

After years in this business, one thing has become obvious to me: **a good instrument is never just hardware — it's hardware, plus application know-how, plus service response.** Customers aren't buying a machine; they're buying the ability to reliably produce results.

AI is the first thing I've seen that can turn manuals, engineers' tacit experience, and scattered service tickets into a capability available on demand.

So my current focus is concrete: **use AI to strip work out of physical businesses** — moving repetitive consulting, product selection, troubleshooting and training off people, so people can focus on the decisions that actually need judgment.
